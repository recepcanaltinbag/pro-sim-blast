Every source this database rests on

This is the project's citation registry, published whole. Each entry carries a DOI that was checked against Crossref, PubMed or the first page of the paper itself, and each one says what it is cited for — so a statement on this site can be followed back to the work it rests on. Nothing here was written from memory: an unverified identifier is worse than no identifier, because a reader who follows it and finds nothing will look for the mistake in themselves.

126 citations, 126 with a DOI, 118 with a PubMed identifier. 118 of them are the reference for a curated type; the rest support a method, a definition or a classification used across the site.

Methods, definitions and classifications

These back statements that are not about one enzyme: the motif definitions the candidate scan applies, the rule that the catalytic carboxylate is required, the classification schemes, and the reviews this project measured itself against — including one whose claim this database contradicts.

  1. Barry SM, Challis GL (2013). Mechanism and Catalytic Diversity of Rieske Non-Heme Iron-Dependent Oxygenases. ACS Catalysis 3:2362-2370
    doi:10.1021/cs400087p PMID 24244861 verified by crossref api
    method check. The review states that the carboxylate of the mononuclear 2-His-1-carboxylate triad can be Asp OR Glu, and that the conserved bridging aspartate is not an iron ligand but a mediator of electron transfer
    on the bridging Asp-to-Glu question the review says of RedG that 'the bridging aspartate residue implicated in electron transfer appears to be mutated to glutamate' and offers no functional interpretation -- the literature precedent for the framing used on this site, 'An open question, not a result.'
  2. De Marco P, Moradas-Ferreira P, Higgins TP, McDonald I, Kenna EM, Murrell JC (1999). Molecular analysis of a novel methanesulfonic acid monooxygenase from the methylotroph Methylosulfonomonas methylovora. J Bacteriol 181:2244-2251
    doi:10.1128/jb.181.7.2244-2251.1999 PMID 10094704 verified by uniprot record
    MsmA (UniProt Q9X404) as a candidate reference; C-S bond cleavage
  3. Higgins TP, De Marco P, Murrell JC (2000). Purification and partial characterization of the hydroxylase component of the methanesulfonic acid monooxygenase. Eur J Biochem 267:4763-4769
    doi:10.1046/j.1432-1327.2000.01538.x PMID 10903510 verified by uniprot record
    purification of the MsmA hydroxylase component -- evidence at the protein level
  4. Kelly DP, Baker SC, Trickett J, Davey M, Murrell JC (1996). Metabolism of methanesulfonic acid involves a multicomponent monooxygenase enzyme. Microbiology 142:251-260
    doi:10.1099/13500872-142-2-251 PMID 8932698 verified by uniprot record
    the demonstration that MsmA is a multicomponent monooxygenase
  5. Kweon O, Kim S, Baek S, Chae J, Adjei MD, Baek D, Kim Y, Cerniglia CE (2008). A new classification system for bacterial Rieske non-heme iron aromatic ring-hydroxylating oxygenases. BMC Biochemistry 9:11
    doi:10.1186/1471-2091-9-11 PMID 18387195 verified by crossref api
    the five-type scheme in the literature (Types I-V), organised by the components of the electron transport chain -- two-component Type I (FNR_C reductase) and Type II (FNR_N reductase), three-component Type III ([2Fe-2S] Fd + FNR_N), Type IV ([2Fe-2S] Fd + GR) and Type V ([3Fe-4S] Fd + GR). Built on 130 enzymes over 25 standard ring-hydroxylating oxygenases
    proposed in place of the Batie scheme, because Batie/Nam cannot account for the [3Fe-4S] ferredoxin or for the genetic separability of the electron transport components
    NOT to be confused with this database's ro_group field. The Kweon axis is the electron partners of the SYSTEM, while ro_group is the sequence clustering of the alpha subunit
  6. Miao H, Schmidt S (2025). Rieske Oxygenases: Powerful Models for Understanding Nature's Orchestration of Electron Transfer and Oxidative Chemistry. Biochemistry 64:3801-3813
    doi:10.1021/acs.biochem.5c00369 verified by local pdf
    definitions of the Rieske motif C-x-H-x(15,47)-C-x(2)-H and of the mononuclear [DE]-x(2)-H-x(3,5)-H motif, which are the gates the candidate scan applies (ro_motif.py)
    the family's reaction repertoire. That C-S bond cleavage occurs in the family but is ABSENT from this set is read from this source, and it is the reason MsmA is proposed as a candidate
  7. Miao H, Oerlemanns R, Hagedoorn PL, Schmidt S (2026). Decoding and Reprogramming Redox Partner Specificity in Rieske Oxygenases for Enhanced Catalytic Activity. bioRxiv (preprint, not peer reviewed)
    doi:10.64898/2026.04.03.713453 verified by local pdf
    the claim that the family shares a ferredoxin partner. This database TESTED that claim and partly CONTRADICTS it; see the disagreements page
  8. Parales RE, Lee K, Resnick SM, Jiang H, Lessner DJ, Gibson DT (2000). Substrate Specificity of Naphthalene Dioxygenase: Effect of Specific Amino Acids at the Active Site of the Enzyme. J. Bacteriol. 182:1641-1649
    doi:10.1128/jb.182.6.1641-1649.2000 verified by crossref api
    evidence that the carboxylate is catalytically REQUIRED. The NDO Asp362-to-Ala mutant gives no product from naphthalene, and neither oxygen consumption nor product is measurable in crude extract although the full-length alpha subunit is produced. This is the basis of the 3/3 rule in triad_gate.py and of section 1 of the method page
  9. Runda M (2024). Rieske oxygenases outside a nutshell. PhD thesis, University of Groningen
    doi:10.33612/diss.1175898687 verified by local pdf
    the Batie classification as given in Table 2 of the thesis, which transcribes it from Batie, Ballou & Correll 1992 (batie_classes.yaml)
