What counts as a genus here, and what is left out

A genus is read from the NCBI taxonomy rank of the genome record that carries the entry: the nearest ancestor whose rank is exactly genus. It is not taken from the first word of the organism name. That difference is measured, not cosmetic: the first word merges every Candidatus organism into one bucket that actually holds 17 separate genera, it carries over quoting and bracketing from the source string ('Nostoc, [Enterobacter]), and it misses renamings — 360 confirmed entries change genus when the rank is used (Sinorhizobium → Ensifer, Rhizobium rhizogenes → Martinezella).

What is left out of every genus count on this page. 867 confirmed entries (4.1%) have no genus-rank ancestor at all in the current NCBI taxonomy. The largest case is Mycolicibacterium, which the organism strings still name and which would be this database's seventh largest “genus”: NCBI now holds it as a subgenus, Mycobacterium (subgen. Mycolicibacterium), and that lineage runs from species straight to the family. Those entries are neither dropped nor given an invented genus; they are carried with the state unassigned and the nearest named rank beside them, and they are excluded from the genus counts shown here. The taxonomy page gives the full measurement.

What qualifies a column, and what the superscript L means

A column qualifies only if it is filled in at least 90 % of members, takes at most four different residues across variants, is dominated by its two commonest residues, and is conserved inside each variant; without those limits the metric simply finds the worst-aligned loops. A superscript L marks a metal-binding position: the motif test accepts a ligand up to two columns away, so a ligand column can still vary here without the centre being incomplete. These are statistically discriminating positions, not structurally verified binding residues, because no structure is used anywhere in this database.
