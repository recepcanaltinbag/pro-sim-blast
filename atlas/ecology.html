Ecology and mobility

Enzymes that degrade industrial compounds have had decades to spread, while enzymes acting on plant-derived aromatics have had hundreds of millions of years to diverge in place. Those two histories leave different traces in the genome: the first on plasmids and beside insertion sequences, the second spread evenly across many genera of one lineage.

3 · Where the organisms were isolated from

Read every number below against this denominator. The isolation source is a free-text field that submitters fill in when they choose to. Of the 11,610 genome records carrying a confirmed alpha subunit, 5,516 state one. Of the 19,125 confirmed alpha subunits, 36.2 % sit on a record with an isolation source, and after normalising that free text into a controlled vocabulary 33.6 % can be placed in a habitat at all. The remainder is not error, it is silence: 12,219 entries have no source recorded and 472 have text that the vocabulary deliberately refuses to guess at, across 186 distinct strings. Those include bare anatomical words that occur in plant, animal and clinical samples alike, ontology accessions with no label, and single words such as "culture" or "tissue". Mis-sorting them would be worse than leaving them out, and 908 of them do carry a host name, which is the sampled organism rather than the environment and so is not used here.

3.2 · Who actually comes from contaminated sites

The expectation is that enzymes acting on industrial compounds are found at industrial sites. Tested on species rather than entries, the expectation holds, but not at the level it is usually stated. Background: 1.7 % of all 3,628 species-by-habitat observations are from a contaminated or industrial site.

4 · What the host field adds, and why it is kept separate

A second ecological field sits in the same GenBank records and answers a different question. The habitat vocabulary asks where the organism was living. The host field asks what it was found in or on. These are not the same thing, and a host name alone does not locate a sample: Homo sapiens is not a habitat, because a bacterium from a human could come from a wound, a gut or a skin swab. For that reason the host is never merged into the habitat vocabulary. It is reported here on its own, through a curated mapping from each host string to a kingdom, so that nothing is inferred beyond what the record states.

5 · Tests

Each association is evaluated twice. The entry level treats every alpha subunit as an observation, which is optimistic because sequencing databases contain many near-identical strains. The genus level keeps one observation per type and genus, which is conservative. A result is called robust only when both levels agree in direction and the genus level remains significant.
