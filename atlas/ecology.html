Ecology and mobility

Enzymes that degrade industrial compounds have had decades to spread, while enzymes acting on plant-derived aromatics have had hundreds of millions of years to diverge in place. Those two histories leave different traces in the genome: the first on plasmids and beside insertion sequences, the second spread evenly across many genera of one lineage.

2 · Taxonomic spread

What counts as a genus here, and what is left out

A genus is read from the NCBI taxonomy rank of the genome record that carries the entry: the nearest ancestor whose rank is exactly genus. It is not taken from the first word of the organism name. That difference is measured, not cosmetic: the first word merges every Candidatus organism into one bucket that actually holds 17 separate genera, it carries over quoting and bracketing from the source string ('Nostoc, [Enterobacter]), and it misses renamings — 360 confirmed entries change genus when the rank is used (Sinorhizobium → Ensifer, Rhizobium rhizogenes → Martinezella).

What is left out of every genus count on this page. 867 confirmed entries (4.1%) have no genus-rank ancestor at all in the current NCBI taxonomy. The largest case is Mycolicibacterium, which the organism strings still name and which would be this database's seventh largest “genus”: NCBI now holds it as a subgenus, Mycobacterium (subgen. Mycolicibacterium), and that lineage runs from species straight to the family. Those entries are neither dropped nor given an invented genus; they are carried with the state unassigned and the nearest named rank beside them, and they are excluded from the genus counts shown here. The taxonomy page gives the full measurement.

3 · Where the organisms were isolated from

Read every number below against this denominator. The isolation source is a free-text field that submitters fill in when they choose to. Of the 12,329 genome records carrying a confirmed alpha subunit, 5,711 state one. Of the 21,046 confirmed alpha subunits, 34.6 % sit on a record with an isolation source, and after normalising that free text into a controlled vocabulary 32.2 % can be placed in a habitat at all. The remainder is not error, it is silence: 13,786 entries have no source recorded and 487 have text that the vocabulary deliberately refuses to guess at, across 187 distinct strings. Those include bare anatomical words that occur in plant, animal and clinical samples alike, ontology accessions with no label, and single words such as "culture" or "tissue". Mis-sorting them would be worse than leaving them out, and 942 of them do carry a host name, which is the sampled organism rather than the environment and so is not used here.

3.2 · Who actually comes from contaminated sites

The expectation is that enzymes acting on industrial compounds are found at industrial sites. Tested on species rather than entries, the expectation holds, but not at the level it is usually stated. Background: 1.6 % of all 4,950 species-by-habitat observations are from a contaminated or industrial site.

4 · What the host field adds, and why it is kept separate

A second ecological field sits in the same GenBank records and answers a different question. The habitat vocabulary asks where the organism was living. The host field asks what it was found in or on. These are not the same thing, and a host name alone does not locate a sample: Homo sapiens is not a habitat, because a bacterium from a human could come from a wound, a gut or a skin swab. For that reason the host is never merged into the habitat vocabulary. It is reported here on its own, through a curated mapping from each host string to a kingdom, so that nothing is inferred beyond what the record states.

5 · Tests

Each association is evaluated twice. The entry level treats every alpha subunit as an observation, which is optimistic because sequencing databases contain many near-identical strains. The genus level keeps one observation per type and genus, which is conservative. A result is called robust only when both levels agree in direction and the genus level remains significant.
