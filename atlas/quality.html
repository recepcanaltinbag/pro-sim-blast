What this database cannot tell you

Every count on this site is a count of sequenced genomes, not of organisms in the world, and several of its numbers depend on conventions rather than measurements. This page gathers those limits in one place, with the size of each effect measured where that is possible.

1 · The same protein, counted many times

Public genome collections contain hundreds of strains of the same species, so identical alpha subunits recur. The database keeps every copy, because the same protein in a different genome sits in a different neighbourhood and genomic context is the subject here. But a percentage over entries is therefore a percentage over sequenced strains.

3 · Does it change the conclusions if the inclusion threshold moves?

The previous section asks whether a result survives the way copies are counted. This one asks a harder question: whether it survives the decision about what counts as a Rieske oxygenase at all. A protein enters this database by covering at least 45 % of its best-scoring profile, and that number was calibrated against a set of known alpha subunits and known non-alpha proteins. Calibration justifies the choice but says nothing about how much the database's own figures depend on it. So the gate is moved and every figure recomputed.

3c · Sigma70 boxes: an enrichment, not a promoter

The −35 and −10 boxes of a bacterial sigma70 promoter are information-poor: six bases each, and common by chance in any AT-rich intergenic stretch. So no promoter is located here and none is predicted — a per-operon claim would not be defensible. What is measurable is enrichment: are the two boxes paired at plausible spacing more often in real upstream regions than in a null built from those same regions?

3b · A second opinion on the call everything rests on

Every number on this site stands on one judgement: that a sequence carries a Rieske [2Fe-2S] domain. That judgement comes from this project's own models. InterPro annotates the same sequences with profiles built by other people, from other data, at other thresholds — so for the first time there is an independent second opinion, and this is what it says.

MeasureSequencesReading
sequences InterPro has annotated at all 25,313 of 70,317; the rest carry no InterPro entry and so do not contradict us — they are unannotated, mostly recent unreviewed records
we call the Rieske cluster intact 25,174 among those InterPro annotated
InterPro agrees on every one of them 25,174 sequences we call Rieske-intact that InterPro does not: 0
InterPro sees a Rieske domain, our gate excludes it 139 all of them fail our four-ligand test
What this does and does not establish. What is independent is what InterPro says about a sequence we handed it. What is not independent is which sequences get asked: the candidate pool was selected with this project's own Rieske and motif models, so InterPro cannot object to a sequence we never included. The measure therefore speaks to precision and says nothing whatever about recall — the sequences this database missed are outside it, and no agreement figure can reach them.

The disagreement runs one way, and it was followed up. 139 sequences carry a Rieske domain by InterPro's profiles but are dropped by this database's gate, which asks for the four cluster ligands rather than for domain similarity. 76 of them carry all three mononuclear iron ligands, which at first reading suggested a substituted cluster ligand on an otherwise working enzyme. Measuring it says otherwise. The canonical cluster-binding motif — C.H.{10,30}C..H, the two C-x-H and C-x-x-H loops that hold the iron — is absent from the raw sequence in 138 of the 139, searched without any alignment or column number at all. So these are not displaced ligands that a window missed: the binding site is not in the sequence. That pattern was validated before being trusted — it matches 3,978 of 4,000 sequences this database does call cluster-intact, and all 76 curated references. The reading is therefore that InterPro places them in the Rieske superfamily on fold similarity while the cluster site itself is gone, and that this database's four-ligand requirement is right to exclude them. It is the strongest external test the gate has had. The comparison and the ligand-by-ligand audit are both downloadable.

4 · Where the reference set repeats itself

Everything above concerns the members. This section concerns the 76 curated enzymes they are compared against, and it is the one unresolved defect in the database. Those 76 records hold 76 distinct sequences, but 4 of them are not uniquely defined: 2 pairs sit at or above 99 % identity. A member then falls to whichever of the two profiles scores fractionally higher. That is arithmetic, not biology, and it is why one page of such a pair can report a member count far below its twin, or none at all. Every such pair carries a recorded curation decision (2 of 2 written down), printed in the table below and repeated on each affected type page, so no reader has to guess whether the duplication was noticed.

Decision: EdoA1 / cumA1 — keep separate, 2026-11-10

The two references carry different substrate labels, ethylbenzene and cumene, because each was assayed on one compound. Merging them would pick one label and discard the other, which asserts more than the assays support: a Rieske oxygenase commonly turns over both. Keeping both records keeps both measurements, and the arbitrary split of members is stated on every affected page.

Decision: CARDO / CarAa — keep separate, 2026-11-10

Both records are separately characterised carbazole 1,9a-dioxygenases in the literature. Merging them would delete one published experimental record to tidy a member count, and the count is already published with its caveat on both pages. The split of members between the two profiles is arbitrary; the two records are not.

Where a type reports no members, and why

7 of the 76 curated types hold no confirmed member. That number on its own is not a finding, because at least three different situations produce it, and they call for opposite responses: the enzyme may be absent from the genomes read here, or it may be present and assigned to a competing profile. The two are told apart by a second, independent measurement — the reference each candidate is closest to by sequence identity, which is computed separately from the HMM assignment that decides the type.

The larger part of this warning is a method defect, not missing data. 6 of the 7 empty types are represented in these genomes and lose every candidate to a competing profile; only 1 are empty because nothing in the data resembles them. Three are empty although an entry identical to their own reference sequence is in the database. The sharpest case is mechanical: for DxnA1 and PbaA1 the two profiles in the assignment library carry a byte-identical probability matrix, although the two reference sequences are only 65.6 % identical. Both family profiles were built from the same four proteins, each set containing the other type's reference, so the two models cannot tell the types apart: every candidate scores the same on both, the tie falls the same way every time, and one type can never recruit anything. Sequence-level redundancy checks do not catch this, because the sequences sit far below any redundancy threshold — the decision passes through the model, not the sequence. Repairing it means rebuilding the profile library and re-assigning every entry, and deciding which homologs belong to which type is a curation judgement rather than something a script should settle. It is published here as a measured defect rather than left as an unexplained zero.

5 · Limits that cannot be measured away

LimitWhat it affectsWhat would resolve it
Substrate labels belong to the reference enzyme, not to its members. Among the curated references, pairs with different substrates reach 99.8 % identity. Every substrate statement on type and entry pages. Biochemical assays on individual members. The database marks how far each member is from the reference so the claim is never implicit. Evidence
Operons are defined by a distance rule, not by transcription. All operon composition percentages. Transcriptomics. In the meantime the rule is tested against a background and the effect of the threshold is tabulated. Operons
Promoters are located, not predicted, and this is a property of the source data rather than a design choice: 88.9 % of the 17,073 downloaded GenBank records are CON records, which declare a length but contain no sequence. The 1,897 records that do carry sequence are eukaryotic transcripts, which have no operons. Everything on the regulation page: the intergenic region is located and measured, but no −35 or −10 box, operator repeat or transcription start can be analysed. Re-fetching the intergenic regions from NCBI. Their coordinates are already stored for 10,186 entries, so the request set is defined; it is the download that is missing. Regulation
Missing partners are not absent partners. Contig ends truncate neighbourhoods and the window is 10 kb. Beta, ferredoxin and reductase percentages are lower bounds. Complete genomes only, at the cost of sample size. Replicon coding-sequence counts are stored so any analysis can restrict itself.
Discriminating residue positions are statistical, not structural. Variant signature tables. Structures or docking. No structure is used anywhere in this database.
Sequencing is biased towards cultured organisms, clinical isolates and model soil bacteria. Every taxonomic and ecological figure. Nothing available here. The taxonomy tree shows the shape of the bias directly. Taxonomy
Most curated substrates carry no individual citation. All 76 types now have a recorded substrate, but for 50 of them chemistry.csv records the source only as established literature on the reference enzyme rather than naming a paper or a structure. Every substrate, product and reaction label, and so every chemistry grouping. The claim is traceable for the 21 types with a paper or a PDB entry recorded, and rests on the curator for the rest. Naming the primary reference for each of those 50 types. Each type page prints the source it has, so the gap is visible per type rather than only in aggregate.
Clustering compares sequences, not function. Variants are sequence-homogeneous subgroups; nothing guarantees that a variant boundary is a functional boundary. Variant counts and the claim that variants are candidate functional units. Experiments. The residue signatures are offered as the strongest available circumstantial evidence.

6 · What is solid

Not everything here is provisional. The selection criterion itself is directly observed rather than inferred: in all 21,046 confirmed entries every one of the four Rieske ligands is present at its aligned position, and the inter-subunit bridging carboxylate is present in 100.0 %. The complete 2-His-1-carboxylate iron triad is present in 86.6 %, and the remainder carry two of the three. Those are measurements on the alignment, not predictions.

The operon rule is likewise supported by a control rather than by assertion: a second alpha subunit in the same window shows no positional enrichment at all (1.12×, p = 0.00 on strand), while beta subunits are 4.02× enriched beside the alpha with a median intergenic gap of 0 base pairs.

ROAR-DB, the Rieske Oxygenase Annotation Resource. Built from RefSeq and GenBank annotation with profile models of 76 Rieske oxygenases characterised in the literature. Every figure states its method and its limits; what the database cannot tell you is documented alongside what it can.
