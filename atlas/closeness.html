What changes when you count only the members whose chemistry is known

Every association on this site is computed over entries, and most entries are distant relatives of a characterised enzyme whose reaction nobody has measured. This page asks how much of each result rests on them. The short answer for the strongest result is reassuring, and for one ecological result it is not.

1 · The bridging carboxylate, by how close the member is

The database reports an association between the inter-subunit bridging carboxylate, aspartate or glutamate, and the enzyme group. Stated as one number over all entries it is V = 0.83. Recomputed within each evidence level it behaves in the way one would hope: it is near-deterministic where the chemistry is known, and it decays with distance.

2 · Where the rule breaks, and what that points at

Within each group the glutamate share is flat across the close tiers and only moves among distant members. Reading along a row shows where the assignment is doing something questionable.

Groups 1 to 4 carry aspartate in essentially every member whose chemistry is known, and glutamate appears only among their distant members. Group 5 is the mirror image: glutamate in almost every close member, decaying with distance. Both patterns say the same thing, that the residue tracks the characterised chemistry and the distant members are where assignment becomes unreliable.

3 · Recompute it yourself

Pick which members to include and the table is recomputed in your browser from the per-entry data, with no server involved. This is the same arithmetic as the rows above, so you can confirm them or ask a question they do not answer.

4 · Who actually comes from contaminated sites

Do members close to a characterised pollutant-degrading enzyme come from contaminated sites more often than their distant relatives, which would be consistent with the family pre-existing in natural environments and specialising later. The prediction is specific: if the family was already present in natural environments and the contaminated-site association came later, then members close to a characterised pollutant degrader should be found at contaminated sites more often than their distant relatives, which should sit in natural habitats.

5 · Is that specialisation, or is it where people looked?

There is a test that separates the two. If the enrichment reflects specialisation to man-made chemistry, it should be confined to enzymes whose substrate is man-made. If it reflects where samples were taken, it should appear for natural-substrate enzymes too.
