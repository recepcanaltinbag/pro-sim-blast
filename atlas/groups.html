What the group numbers mean

A group here is a clustering of alpha subunit sequences in this set, and nothing else. It is not a classification of the enzyme system, it says nothing about the domain of life, and it is not a reduced form of any scheme in the literature. The numbers are internal identifiers: this page exists so that a number on another page can be read as a definition rather than guessed at.

These are not Batie's five classes, and the shared count of five is a coincidence. Batie's scheme partitions the electron transport chain — the type of the reductase and of the ferredoxin — so it is a property of the whole multi-component system in a given strain, not of the alpha subunit. The two schemes cut the space differently, and the clearest demonstration is in the data: Batie class IB contains both OMO and BZDO, which here are 1_101_OxoO in group 1 and 4_411_XylX in group 4. One class, two groups. A Batie class is also not a property of the enzyme: CARDO appears in three different classes depending on the strain, which is why no Batie column is published on the type pages of this site. Kweon's five types (2008) are a third, separate axis, again built on electron transport components. This database assigns neither, and claims nothing about either.

21,046 entries are distributed over 7 groups. Two of them are not literature groups and are never published as numbers — they are shown as ungrouped everywhere on the site, and they appear here with their reason, because a reader who meets the word "ungrouped" is owed the measurement that put them there.

Why each group is a group

group 5 — Quaternary amine oxygenases

The cleanest group in the set: all eight types act on quaternary amines, and not one member carries a beta subunit. It also holds the plant choline monooxygenases, which is why membership of a group is not a statement about the domain of life.

ungrouped (plant chlorophyll ROs: PAO, CAO, PTC52) — Plastid chlorophyll-pathway oxygenases

Added 2026-10-14. Published as “ungrouped (plant chlorophyll ROs: PAO, CAO, PTC52)”.

Added for pheophorbide a oxygenase, chlorophyllide a oxygenase and PTC52 -- plastid enzymes of chlorophyll metabolism. Separated on ALIGNABILITY, not on domain of life. No existing reference aligns to them beyond the Rieske domain: the best hits cover only 30-34 % of the query, which is the sequence-level form of what the carboxylate audit found structurally, where a C-terminal catalytic-lobe probe scored on 2.7 % of this set against 57.7 % of ordinary members. Folding them into group 1 would redefine that group's alignment columns using sequences that cannot be aligned over the catalytic lobe, which is the exact fault that made 1,388 carboxylates read as absent when they were present. They are 23-32 % identical to one another over 57-87 % of their length, so they form a family rather than a set of singletons, and none carries a beta subunit. NOT called "the eukaryotic group" on purpose: CmoS, CmoB and CmoA are eukaryotic and belong to group 5, and Nvd and DAF-36 are animal sterol desaturases that align full length to group 3. A label by domain of life would contradict the data.

ungrouped (AerC) — Long Rieske oxygenases with an inserted catalytic lobe

Added 2026-10-21. Published as “ungrouped (AerC)”.

Added for AerC (7_701_AerC), the aeruginosin-pathway Rieske oxygenase of Kalymmatonema gypsitolerans NIES-4073, which performs oxidative carbocyclisation and C-C bond cleavage in one active site (doi:10.1021/jacs.5c01994). Separated on ALIGNABILITY, the same ground as group 6 and a more extreme case of it. AerC is 717 aa, far outside the 330-460 aa range of the other references, and the Rieske domain sits at residue 146 while the mononuclear site sits at 511; in an ordinary alpha subunit that spacing is about 80 to 210. There is a large insertion between the two, so a global alignment to the catalytic-centre model covers only 18.9 % of the model and stops at column 231, never reaching the catalytic columns. Folding AerC into any existing group would redefine that group's alignment columns using a sequence that cannot be aligned across the catalytic lobe. THE GROUP IS NOT A SINGLETON, and this was measured before the type was admitted. A single-sequence AerC profile run against the 26,693 candidate proteins passes the 0.45 coverage gate on 18 of them and covers those 18 at 0.84-1.00 (median 0.98). Those same 18 are currently assigned to 5_507_BmoA (426 aa) and 3_311_PhtAa (462 aa) at coverage 0.21-0.33, that is over roughly a fifth of their length. So admitting AerC does not take members on a technicality; it repairs 18 assignments whose present reference matches a fragment of the protein. The Nodularia sphaerocarpa orthologue ULP74381.1 (720 aa, SuoC) carries the same ligands at the same columns. OPEN: the mononuclear carboxylate is present on biochemical grounds (the paper measures 3.00 +/- 0.02 Fe per monomer, that is 2 Fe Rieske plus 1 Fe mononuclear) but its RESIDUE could not be located, because the model region that holds that column has no counterpart in AerC. Recorded as roadmap item 68; see analysis_out/aerc_resolution.json. members and beta_rate are null until the pipeline has been rerun with this reference in the library; they are not guesses.

Whether these groups are recovered as clades is a different question, and it is measured rather than asserted: see Phylogeny, where only group 5 forms a single clade and its bootstrap support falls from 0.995 among the references to 0.49 in the larger set.
