What can be predicted, and what only looks predictable

A model trained on this database can reach high accuracy without having learned anything, because the answer is often sitting in the training set as a near-identical copy. This page measures that effect rather than hiding it, and the measurement turns out to be the most useful result on the page.

1 · The same model, split two ways

Each row is one prediction task. The honest column holds out whole enzyme types, so a model must generalise to a type it has never seen. The leaked column splits entries at random, which lets near-identical sequences of the same type sit on both sides of the split.

2 · The clearest demonstration

A nearest-neighbour classifier learns nothing at all. It copies the label of the most similar sequence it was shown. On a random split that is almost perfect, because nearly every test sequence has a near-twin of the same type in training.

None of this makes them binding residues. Being near the metal in the alignment is not the same as lining the pocket, and the structural page is where pocket membership is actually measured.

4 · What this cannot support

  • no claim about a reaction class represented by 2 or 3 types (C_N_cleavage, O_demethylation, angular_dioxygenation): under the grouped scheme those classes have one or two test folds in total
  • no claim that the columns reported here are substrate-binding pocket residues. There is no structure in this database; what was measured is which alignment columns separate the classes
  • no claim that a residue identity explains the chemistry rather than marking the clade. Reaction class and substrate class are both strongly structured by HMM group, so a sequence model can reach its accuracy by recognising the clade alone; the clade-oracle baseline is there precisely to make that visible
  • no causal claim in either direction for question 3. Operon composition and substrate class are both properties of a lineage with a history, and a shared genome is not evidence of a shared pathway
  • no claim that the curated labels are correct. Most entries sit in the distant or novel evidence tiers, where the reaction is unknown and was inherited from the type. This module treats the label as given
  • no usable sequence-to-substrate predictor for a new protein. The grouped accuracies here are far from a decision rule, and the reference-pair calibration already showed that no global identity threshold guarantees the same substrate
