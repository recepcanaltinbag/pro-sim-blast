Phylogeny of the catalytic core

Maximum-likelihood tree of the 1,276 tips: the 71 curated reference enzymes, drawn as squares in bold, and one representative sequence per sequence-homogeneous variant. Branch lengths are substitutions per site in the aligned Rieske-plus-catalytic core.

Not every variant has a tip, and the rule is two-ended. A variant with a single member is its own representative. A variant with 5 or more members contributes the medoid of its members. Variants in between, with two to four members, are skipped, because a medoid of so few sequences is not a stable choice. The consequence is measurable: 1,205 of 1,809 variants appear here, and the 604 that do not hold 1,631 entries, or 14 % of the database. Those entries are present everywhere else on the site; they have no tip on this tree. Four types have no representative at all under this rule (OxoO, TDO, DxnA1, NDO (314)), so they appear only as their reference square.

How to read this tree. A reference embedded in a tight clade of tips of its own colour is a type that is well sampled by close relatives, and its substrate label is informative for that clade. A reference on a long branch, or a clade whose tips are coloured for a different type than the nearest reference, marks a region where profile assignment and phylogeny disagree; there, type membership carries no functional claim. Deep nodes separating the colour blocks correspond to the historical RO groups, which this tree recovers from sequence alone.

ROAR-DB, the Rieske Oxygenase Annotation Resource. Built from RefSeq and GenBank annotation with profile models of 71 Rieske oxygenases characterised in the literature. Every figure states its method and its limits; what the database cannot tell you is documented alongside what it can.
