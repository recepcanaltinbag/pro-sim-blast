The active site, and how much of it varies

Every page before this one reasons about sequence. This one puts the sequence into three dimensions: which residues actually line the pocket around the catalytic iron, and which of them change between the members of a type.

1 · Telling the two irons apart

A Rieske oxygenase carries two iron sites and they must not be confused. The Rieske centre is an iron pair bridged by inorganic sulfur, held by two cysteines and two histidines; the catalytic centre is a single iron held by two histidines and a carboxylate, with no sulfur. They were separated by that geometry rather than by trusting the file's labels, in all 17 types that have a structure.

The four types that need the search window are the OxoO, OMO, CARDO and CarAa group. The profile had always tolerated an odd residue at one catalytic column in those four; the crystals show why. Their second catalytic histidine sits one column earlier, so it is an alignment shift in that subfamily rather than a missing residue.

2 · Pockets for the types with no crystal, and whether they can be trusted

Most types have never been crystallised. Their structures were not folded here; AlphaFold has already modelled them, and each model was accepted only when its sequence matched the curated one character for character. A predicted model contains no metal, so the pocket cannot be found by asking what sits near the iron. It is placed instead at the centre of the catalytic triad, which alignment locates reliably.

Read the bottom of the table first. In the largest and most heterogeneous types almost nothing but the metal ligands is fixed. That is the structural counterpart of what the closeness page shows from sequence alone: a type is a region of sequence space, and only the residues that hold the iron are common to all of it. A column that varies is not necessarily functional, because most members are distant relatives whose reaction is unknown, so variation there may be heterogeneity rather than tuning.

4 · Are pocket residues more conserved than the rest of the protein?

The obvious answer is yes, and the obvious answer is a tautology. Membership of this database requires all four Rieske ligands and at least two of the three iron ligands, so those residues are invariant because an entry lacking them was never admitted. They sit inside the pocket, so including them measures the admission rule rather than biology.

5 · What was wrong with the variant comparison

Two separate faults were conflated. First, the number shown next to a variant, leaf.median_identity, is the median pairwise identity WITHIN that variant, that is how similar its own members are. It is not a between-variant quantity, so placing two such numbers side by side does not compare the two variants at all. Second, even a correct between-variant global identity is a weak measure in this family: curated pairs with different substrates reach 99.8 % identity, so substrate choice is not set by global similarity. Both numbers are reported here, computed on the same alignment with the same gap rule, so they can finally be read against each other.

Pairs that are similar overall but differ in the pocket are the interesting case, and they are rare here, for a reason that is methodological rather than biological: variants are created by splitting a type until each piece is internally homogeneous, so two different variants of one type are by construction seldom very similar. The sample removes the candidates before the comparison begins.

7 · What this cannot tell you

  • Only 17 of the 71 curated types carry a PDB identifier, and those 17 types come from 13 distinct structures, so nothing here generalises to the other types by itself.
  • Pocket residues are read from one crystal conformation. Side chains move when a substrate binds, so a residue just outside the cut-off is not evidence that it never contacts substrate.
  • The pocket is defined by distance to the iron, not by a bound substrate. Where a structure contains no substrate or inhibitor, the pocket is the cavity around the metal and not a measured binding site.
  • Residue distributions at pocket columns describe the members assigned to that type by the profile library. Type assignment is a nearest-reference statement and about three quarters of members sit at distant or novel evidence tiers, so a varying pocket column may reflect that heterogeneity rather than a functional difference.
  • Group comparisons of pocket composition have one to four types per group, after de-duplication even fewer. They are descriptive; no test is reported because none would be meaningful at that n.
  • The acidic count of a pocket is dominated by residues that are conserved in the whole family rather than by anything substrate-specific: the iron carboxylate at column 355 is an aspartate in every resolved structure and column 354 carries an acidic or amide residue in most of them. Net pocket charge therefore mostly reports the conserved catalytic region, which is a further reason not to read it as a specificity signal.
  • No binding energy, specificity prediction or substrate docking is attempted, and none can be supported by this data.
  • Pockets in the 'predicted_structures' section are inferred from computed models, not observed. They are kept separate from the crystal-derived pockets throughout and must not be merged with them. Side-chain placement is the least reliable part of a structure prediction, and the pocket is exactly a set of side chains, so a predicted pocket residue is weaker evidence than a crystallographic one even when its pLDDT is high.
