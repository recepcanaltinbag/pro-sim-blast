Atlas of the Rieske oxygenase family

A Rieske oxygenase (RO) alpha subunit carries two metal centres: a Rieske [2Fe-2S] cluster that receives electrons and a mononuclear Fe(II) site where the substrate is oxidised. This atlas describes every protein in the database that demonstrably has both, together with the genes around it, the partners that feed it electrons, the regulator upstream of its operon, and the organisms that carry it.

Three units, three different claims. A type is one of the 71 enzymes characterised in the literature and curated here; its substrate is experimental fact. A member is a protein assigned to the type whose profile it scores highest against; this states closest characterised relative, not identical function. A variant is a sequence-homogeneous subgroup of members, the finest unit at which a distinct function can reasonably be proposed. Pages that show substrates always show the evidence level alongside, because in this family references with different substrates reach 99 % identity to each other.

Sections

Phylogeny

Maximum-likelihood tree of the curated references and one representative per variant, built from the aligned catalytic core. Shows which types are lineages and which are arbitrary cuts through a continuum.

Sequence space

Similarity network and type-versus-type identity matrix, with the identities between characterised references as the calibration for any threshold.

Taxonomy

Phylum composition of every type, including the plant, fungal and animal branches that pass the same catalytic-centre test as the bacterial enzymes.

Operons and electron transport

Beta subunits, ferredoxins and reductases detected from neighbour protein sequences rather than annotation text, per type and per RO group.

Regulation

The 5' end of each operon, the intergenic region where its promoter must lie, and the regulator family facing it.

Ecology and mobility

Plasmid and transposon association against substrate class, and taxonomic spread as a proxy for how long a type has been inherited vertically.

Novel candidates

Confirmed oxygenases that no characterised enzyme explains, grouped by variant, with the disagreement between the two independent measures of novelty.

Evidence

How close each member actually is to a characterised enzyme, and which types consist almost entirely of distant relatives.

Statistics

Each association tested twice, once per entry and once per genus, with effect sizes and an explicit verdict on robustness.
