2 · How the strict set is defined

A protein enters this list only if all of the following hold, so that annotation noise and one-off sequencing artefacts are excluded:
  • confirmed entry: all four Rieske ligands and a complete 2-His-1-carboxylate triad;
  • at least 300 residues, so fragments cannot qualify;
  • under 25 % identity to every curated reference protein;
  • a member of a variant with at least three members, so the sequence is reproduced in more than one genome rather than standing alone.
That leaves {{ n_total|fmt }} proteins in {{ variants|length }} variants.

3 · Two definitions of novelty, and where they disagree

This database measures novelty twice, by different means. The evidence level compares each protein with the reference proteins directly. The assignment class asks whether the protein is typical of the cluster it was placed in, by comparing it with the consensus of its own subfamily. A protein can be the solid core of its own subfamily and still be far from anything characterised, which is exactly the case worth looking at.

6 · What would settle it

Nothing here is a functional claim. A candidate becomes an enzyme with a known reaction only through experiment, and the most informative next steps are the ones this database can point at: the variants with the most members and the most complete operons, because a conserved gene neighbourhood is independent evidence that the protein is used. Sequences are provided so that any of them can be synthesised and tested.
