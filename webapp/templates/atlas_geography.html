Where these enzymes have been seen

{{ geo.coverage.entries_with_a_country|fmt }} of {{ geo.coverage.confirmed_entries|fmt }} confirmed alpha subunits ({{ '%.0f %%' % (100 * geo.coverage.share) }}) come from a record that states where the sample was collected, across {{ geo.coverage.distinct_places }} distinct places. That is enough to draw a map. Whether the map means anything is a separate question, and it is the one this page is organised around.

This is in large part a map of sequencing capacity. A country here is where a genome was collected and deposited, not where the enzyme lives. The United States alone carries {{ '%.0f %%' % (100 * geo.usa_share) }} of the entries that have a country at all, which says more about who runs sequencing centres than about Rieske oxygenases. Three numbers are therefore given for every place and they answer different questions: raw entries (most biased), distinct genera (strain duplicates removed), and the deviation of each enzyme type from the overall distribution — the last being the only one from which anything is inferred, because sampling effort appears on both sides of that comparison and largely cancels.

Does any enzyme type come from a different set of places?

Comparing one type's country distribution against the country distribution of all confirmed entries removes most of the sampling bias, because the same bias is in both. The test is the one used throughout the statistics page: a standardised residual per cell, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected, reported only when the difference is also at least ten percentage points, and repeated after collapsing every type, genus and country to a single observation.

Where the main variant sits, and where the others do

Within a type, entries that share an identical sequence variant are one observation of one thing. Comparing where the most common variant was collected against where the remaining variants were collected asks whether a type's diversity sits in one place or is spread out. The same caveat applies throughout: a variant found only in one country may simply be a variant only one country has sequenced.

How this was built

Locations come from the /geo_loc_name or /country qualifier of the source record, stored verbatim and split at the first colon. 121 distinct strings appear; {{ geo.coverage.places_on_the_map }} map onto a country code, and the rest are oceans, seas and two dissolved states, listed above rather than discarded.
