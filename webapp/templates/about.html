Methods

1. What counts as a Rieske oxygenase alpha subunit

Rieske-domain proteins (Pfam PF00355) include many proteins that are not oxygenases: ferredoxins, cytochrome bc1/b6f iron–sulfur proteins, NirD and others. An E-value threshold cannot separate them, because their Rieske domains are genuinely homologous. ROAR-DB therefore requires the full architecture:

  1. Profile coverage. 71 experimentally characterised RO alpha subunits were curated from the literature (two originally included sequences, IsoMO and CdnD, were removed because they lack the Rieske centre or the catalytic domain). Each seeds a profile HMM built from its close homologues. A protein must cover ≥ 45 % of the best-scoring model (calibrated on 2,788 full-length alphas and 1,570 known non-alphas: 99.8 % sensitivity, 95.8 % rejection).
  2. Catalytic-centre residues. The protein is aligned to a 71-reference motif model and checked at fixed columns for the four Rieske ligands (Cys-X-His … Cys-X-X-His, all required) and the mononuclear-iron 2-His-1-carboxylate triad (≥ 2 of 3 within ±2 columns).
  3. Type assignment. The reference model with the highest bit score gives the reference type (cluster); the first digit of the model name is the historical RO group.

2. Genomic context

Every GenBank record was scanned directly for CDS translations carrying the Rieske ligand signature, candidates were tested as above, and all CDS within ±10 kb were stored with strand, signed distance and gene offset (origin-spanning genes on circular replicons are handled). Neighbour products are categorised by keyword (regulator, transposon, β subunit, ferredoxin, reductase, transporter, ring cleavage …) and regulator families are recorded separately.

3. Operons

Annotation text is unreliable for operon components (many are "hypothetical protein"). The neighbour protein sequences are therefore searched with Pfam models: Ring_hydroxyl_B (β subunit), Fer2 / Rieske / Rieske_2 (ferredoxin, ≤ 250 aa), NAD_binding_1 / FAD_binding_6 / FAD_binding_8 / Pyr_redox_2 / Pyr_redox_dim (FNR- and GR-type reductases). An operon is the run of same-strand genes around the alpha subunit with intergenic gaps ≤ 150 bp. Component presence is reported both inside the operon and anywhere within ±10 kb.

4. Variants and novelty

Within each reference type, members were split recursively (CD-HIT at decreasing identity) until leaves reached a median pairwise identity target; leaves are reported as variants with their genus composition and distinguishing neighbours. Each member's identity to its nearest reference sequence gives an assignment class: core, divergent, or novel_candidate (confidently an RO alpha, but far from every reference type).

6. Known limits

  • Substrate labels are per reference type and come from the literature for the reference enzyme; members of a type may act on different substrates.
  • Operon prediction uses a simple distance rule; it does not model promoters or terminators.
  • Contig ends truncate neighbourhoods; entries on short contigs may appear to lack components.
  • Entries that share an identical RefSeq protein (WP_ accession) across genomes are kept as separate genomic entries.
