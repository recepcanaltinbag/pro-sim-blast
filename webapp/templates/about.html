Methods

This database is built from GenBank genome records and a set of 71 Rieske oxygenase alpha subunits characterised in the literature. Every step below is a script in the repository, and every table it produces is downloadable from the page that displays it.

1 · What qualifies as a Rieske oxygenase alpha subunit

Proteins carrying a Rieske [2Fe-2S] domain, Pfam PF00355, include many that are not oxygenases: ferredoxins, the iron–sulfur proteins of the cytochrome bc1 and b6f complexes, nitrite reductase subunits and others. An expectation-value threshold cannot separate them, because their Rieske domains are genuinely homologous and score just as well. We measured this: in a test set of several hundred proteins of each kind, every member of both groups passed a 10−10 cut-off. What distinguishes an oxygenase is not the Rieske domain but the mononuclear Fe(II) catalytic site that accompanies it.

Two independent criteria are therefore required.

  1. Profile coverage. Each of the 71 curated references seeds a profile hidden Markov model built from its close homologues; these models span the Rieske domain and the catalytic domain together. A protein must cover at least 45 % of the best-scoring model. The threshold was calibrated on 2,788 full-length alpha subunits and 1,570 proteins known not to be alpha subunits, retaining 99.8 % of the former while rejecting 95.8 % of the latter. Raising it to 80 % improves rejection by less than two points but loses a fifth of true positives.
  2. Catalytic-centre residues. The protein is aligned to a motif model built from the same references, and specific match-state columns are checked directly: the four Rieske ligands Cys-X-His and Cys-X-X-His, all four required, and the 2-His-1-carboxylate triad of the mononuclear iron site, at least two of three within a two-column window. The window tolerates sub-family alignment shifts, which otherwise reject five of the curated references themselves.

Two sequences originally in the reference set were removed during this work: one lacks a Rieske centre altogether and belongs to the soluble di-iron monooxygenase family, the other has the Rieske centre but no catalytic site and is an electron-transfer component. Both had been propagating into the classification as if they were oxygenases.

2 · Type assignment, and what it does not mean

Each confirmed protein is assigned to the reference type whose profile it scores highest against, by bit score rather than expectation value, since the latter depends on sequence length and database size. The assignment names the closest characterised relative. It is not a functional annotation, and the database never presents it as one.

The reason is measurable within the reference set itself. Among the curated enzymes, pairs with different experimentally determined substrates reach nearly 100 % identity to one another, while pairs sharing a substrate fall to roughly 32 %. Substrate selection in this family is determined by a few active-site positions, so no global identity threshold can certify it. Every member therefore carries an evidence level stating how close it actually is to a characterised enzyme, and substrate labels are shown together with that level.

3 · Genomic context

Rather than locating proteins by translated search and recording coordinates alone, each genome record is scanned directly for coding sequences whose translation carries the Rieske ligand signature. Candidates are then tested as in section 1. For every confirmed protein, all coding sequences within 10 kb on either side are stored with their strand, signed distance and gene offset. Genes spanning the origin of a circular replicon are handled explicitly, since treating their coordinates naively makes a short gene appear to span the whole replicon and become a neighbour of everything on it. No keyword filter is applied at extraction: neighbours annotated as "hypothetical protein" are exactly the ones a context database should be able to characterise, so they are kept and classified at query time.

4 · Operons and electron-transport partners

Annotation text is unreliable for the partners that matter here, so the protein sequences of all stored neighbours are searched with Pfam profiles: Ring_hydroxyl_B for the beta subunit, Rieske or Fer2 domains in proteins under 250 residues for Rieske-type and plant-type ferredoxins, and FAD/NAD-binding or pyridine-nucleotide-disulfide domains for FNR-type and glutathione-reductase-type reductases. An operon is defined as the run of same-strand genes around the alpha subunit with intergenic gaps of at most 150 base pairs. Partner presence is reported both inside that run and anywhere within the 10 kb window, and both are lower bounds: contig ends and distant loci hide partners that exist.

5 · Regulation

For every confirmed protein the 5' end of its predicted operon is determined from the strand, and the intergenic distance to the nearest upstream gene is measured. That interval is where the promoter must lie. The upstream gene's orientation separates a shared, divergent promoter region from a possible read-through arrangement, and its product name assigns a regulator family where one can be recognised. The promoter sequence is not analysed, and the reason lies in the source data rather than in the design of this database: 88.9 % of the GenBank records used here are CON records, which state a sequence length but contain no sequence at all. No boxes, operator repeats or transcription start sites can be inferred from this material. Results

6 · Variants and novelty

Within each type, members are split recursively at decreasing identity until each subgroup reaches a median pairwise identity target. The resulting leaves are reported as variants, each with its genus composition, plasmid rate and distinguishing neighbours. A variant is the finest unit at which a distinct function can reasonably be proposed; several types that look homogeneous at the type level separate into variants with clearly different genomic contexts.

Known limitation: the clustering step operates on match-state columns of the alignment, so insertions and fused extra domains are excised before sequences are compared. A protein with a large additional domain can therefore group with a plain enzyme. Pairwise identities quoted on variant pages share this definition.

7 · Phylogeny and sequence space

The 71 references and one representative per variant are aligned to the catalytic-centre model; columns with gaps in more than half the sequences are discarded, leaving the conserved core, and the tree is inferred with FastTree. Separately, an all-against-all search provides a similarity network and a type-against-type identity matrix. Together these show which types are coherent lineages and which are arbitrary cuts through a continuum. Tree · Network

8 · Statistics

Alpha subunits are not independent observations: sequence databases contain many near-identical strains of the same species. Every association test is therefore run twice, once per entry and once after collapsing the data to one observation per type and genus, and a result is reported as robust only when both agree. Effect sizes accompany every p value. Results

10 · Remaining limitations

  • Substrate labels belong to the reference enzyme of each type, not to its members.
  • Operon boundaries come from a distance rule, without promoter or terminator models.
  • Contig ends truncate neighbourhoods, so missing partners are not evidence of absence.
  • Sequenced genomes are heavily biased towards cultured organisms, which shapes every taxonomic and ecological figure in this database.
  • Several types have no substrate recorded because the original publications were not available; they are shown as unknown rather than guessed.
