2 · Regulators diverge faster than the enzymes they control

For pairs of entries within one enzyme type, the identity between the two alpha subunits can be compared with the identity between their two regulators. The comparison is restricted to pairs whose regulators belong to the same family, because two regulators of different families are different proteins and a low identity between them measures replacement rather than divergence.

3 · The same chemistry under different control

A regulator can be replaced rather than merely diverge. Comparing the regulator family at both ends of a pair answers how often that happens.

At near-identical enzymes the regulator family is always the same; below 60 % identity it differs in about seven pairs out of ten. So as an enzyme type spreads and diverges, the same chemistry repeatedly comes under a different kind of transcriptional control. This is also why the unrestricted comparison overstates regulator divergence, and why section 2 excludes these pairs.

4 · Mobility, and how much of it is an annotation artefact

The database has reported that enzymes acting on man-made substrates are more often mobile. That claim rested on a variable defined as "on a plasmid or with a transposon nearby", and the transposon half is a regular expression over the GenBank product text, which measures how a genome was annotated. Of {{ rel.totals.get('confirmed', 0)|fmt }} confirmed entries, {{ mob.regex_only|fmt }} are counted mobile only because of that text match. So the claim was recomputed three ways.
