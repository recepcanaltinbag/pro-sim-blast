Operon architecture and electron transport

A Rieske oxygenase cannot work alone. Electrons travel from NAD(P)H through a reductase, usually via a small ferredoxin, into the Rieske centre of the alpha subunit, and many alphas additionally assemble with a beta subunit into an α₃β₃ hexamer. This page reports which of those partners are actually encoded next to each alpha, and in what order.

Partners are identified by sequence, not by annotation text. Product names in GenBank call a large share of these genes "hypothetical protein", so counting annotation keywords measures vocabulary rather than biology. Every neighbour protein sequence was extracted from the genome record and searched with Pfam profiles: Ring_hydroxyl_B for the beta subunit, Rieske or Fer2 under 250 residues for ferredoxins, and FAD/NAD-binding or pyridine-nucleotide-disulfide domains for reductases. A missing partner is not proof of absence: contig ends, genes outside the 10 kb window and unsequenced regions all hide partners, so the percentages below are lower bounds.
