Predicted Rieske oxygenases in archaea and fungi

No archaeal or fungal Rieske oxygenase has ever been characterised experimentally. This page is what sequence analysis finds anyway: {{ pr.counts.archaeal }} archaeal and {{ pr.counts.fungal }} fungal proteins that carry the complete catalytic apparatus of the family. Not one of them has been purified, assayed, or shown to turn over a substrate. They are predictions, and the page is built so that they cannot be mistaken for anything else.

Nothing on this page is part of the curated set. {{ pr.rule }}. No prediction is assigned an ro_group, none seeds a profile, and none is counted in any figure, ratio or q-value elsewhere in this database. A prediction moves into the curated set only when experimental evidence is published, and then it passes the ordinary admission gates rather than living here.

1 · Why the test can be believed

The claim rests on one thing: a protein is called a Rieske oxygenase only if all four Rieske ligands and the mononuclear 2-His-1-carboxylate triad sit at the model's derived columns. The discriminating power of that rule was measured in the same run, on controls, and the negative controls are the important half.

But not one lineage either

This was the result that did not go as expected. The tree holds the 109 archaeal sequences, the curated references and 281 bacterial Rieske oxygenases. The archaeal proteins do not form a single clade: they split into {{ pr.archaea.phylogeny.n_clusters }} pure clusters, each well supported within itself, and each with a bacterial sister group. Halobacteria alone shows at least two independent acquisitions.
