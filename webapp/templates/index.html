Rieske oxygenases: one iron architecture, many chemistries

Rieske oxygenases use O2 to do oxidations that are otherwise chemically hard. One alpha subunit chain holds both halves of the machine: a Rieske [2Fe-2S] cluster that delivers the electrons, and a mononuclear Fe(II) site that does the chemistry.

One of these columns does not read what it claims to read. Electrons cross the subunit interface, so the two carboxylates of this fold are different sites: one coordinates the iron, one relays electrons to a neighbour. Tested against 27 curated crystal structures, the bridging column passes — the residue it calls sits 5.9–7.2 Å from the neighbour's Rieske cluster. The catalytic column fails: the residue it calls is 10.9–14.7 Å from the iron in 26 of 26 testable structures, so no member counts are published for it. That is why membership requires two of three iron ligands rather than all three. The measurement, the structures and the open question →
