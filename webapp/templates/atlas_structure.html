1 · Telling the two irons apart

A Rieske oxygenase carries two iron sites and they must not be confused. The Rieske centre is an iron pair bridged by inorganic sulfur, held by two cysteines and two histidines; the catalytic centre is a single iron held by two histidines and a carboxylate, with no sulfur. They were separated by that geometry rather than by trusting the file's labels, in all {{ site.coverage.types_with_catalytic_iron_resolved }} types that have a structure.

2 · Pockets for the types with no crystal, and whether they can be trusted

Most types have never been crystallised. Their structures were not folded here; AlphaFold has already modelled them, and each model was accepted only when its sequence matched the curated one character for character. A predicted model contains no metal, so the pocket cannot be found by asking what sits near the iron. It is placed instead at the centre of the catalytic triad, which alignment locates reliably.

Read the bottom of the table first. In the largest and most heterogeneous types almost nothing but the metal ligands is fixed. That is the structural counterpart of what the closeness page shows from sequence alone: a type is a region of sequence space, and only the residues that hold the iron are common to all of it. A column that varies is not necessarily functional, because most members are distant relatives whose reaction is unknown, so variation there may be heterogeneity rather than tuning.
