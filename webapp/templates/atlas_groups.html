What the group numbers mean

A group here is a clustering of alpha subunit sequences in this set, and nothing else. It is not a classification of the enzyme system, it says nothing about the domain of life, and it is not a reduced form of any scheme in the literature. The numbers are internal identifiers: this page exists so that a number on another page can be read as a definition rather than guessed at.

These are not Batie's five classes, and the shared count of five is a coincidence. Batie's scheme partitions the electron transport chain — the type of the reductase and of the ferredoxin — so it is a property of the whole multi-component system in a given strain, not of the alpha subunit. The two schemes cut the space differently, and the clearest demonstration is in the data: Batie class IB contains both OMO and BZDO, which here are 1_101_OxoO in group 1 and 4_411_XylX in group 4. One class, two groups. A Batie class is also not a property of the enzyme: CARDO appears in three different classes depending on the strain, which is why no Batie column is published on the type pages of this site. Kweon's five types (2008) are a third, separate axis, again built on electron transport components. This database assigns neither, and claims nothing about either.
