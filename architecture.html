The shared architecture, as measured

Every entry in this database carries the same two metal centres. This page is the measurement behind that claim: which alignment columns are read, which of them were tested against crystal structures, and which one failed that test. It is the methodological core of the set, and it is deliberately long.

2 · Two carboxylates, and they are not the same kind of site

Calling both of these "the carboxylate" obscures that they sit in different shells. In naphthalene dioxygenase the mononuclear iron is coordinated by His208 at 2.04 Å, His213 at 2.15 Å and Asp362 at 2.27 Å: that is the 2-His-1-carboxylate triad, and its carboxylate is a first-shell ligand. The bridging Asp205 is 6.95 Å from the same iron and coordinates nothing at all; it is a second-shell relay, hydrogen-bonded to His208 on one side and, across the subunit junction, to the Rieske ligand His104 of the neighbour. One holds the metal, the other carries electrons to it. They are listed together below only because both are carboxylates read from an alignment column — and the audit of those columns came out differently for the two, which is the point of the table.

The column test passed only because an acidic residue does sit there — it asks whether the column holds a D or an E, not whether that residue coordinates the metal. Yes. In the reference set The iron-coordinating carboxylate is aspartate in 26 of 26 structures. It lands on column 431 in 18 of them and on 432 in 2 more, with the remaining 6 spread over 407–411 and 449–451, so no single column holds them all. 66 Asp, 3 Glu, 7 neither (Cys or Ser) of 76

Both columns were checked against structures, and they came out differently. The bridging column passes: in the 9 structures where the neighbouring subunit is present in the file, the residue it calls sits 5.9–7.2 Å from that neighbour's Rieske cluster and 6.7–10.6 Å from its own subunit's iron in 8 of 9, which is the geometry of a bridge and nothing else. For naphthalene dioxygenase it returns Asp205, the residue whose carboxylate is hydrogen-bonded to a Rieske ligand on one side and an iron ligand on the other. The catalytic column fails: the residue it calls is 10.9–14.7 Å from the iron in 26 of 26 testable structures. The two mononuclear histidine columns pass as well — for the phthalate dioxygenase they give His181 and His186, exactly the residues that coordinate the iron in the crystal, at 2.01 and 1.94 Å. So membership requiring two of the three iron ligands is no longer a tolerance for alignment shift but a necessity: the third ligand is not something a fixed column can report. The measurement also reproduces a known number, which is the best check that it is reading the right thing: in the structures that hold only one subunit the nearest Rieske cluster comes out 44–48 Å away, and the distance from the iron to its own subunit's Rieske centre is reported as 43.5 Å, against 12 Å across the subunit junction. The bridge reaches the neighbour, not its own cluster.

An open question, not a result. In the quaternary-amine branch this position is not aspartate: carnitine oxygenase has Glu205 and the stachydrine demethylase has Cys202. Neither is a Rieske ligand — the cluster in both structures is held by the usual Cys86/Cys106/His88/His109, and these residues sit 46 and 48 Å from it — and both are 6.9 and 6.0 Å from their own mononuclear iron, the same distance the verified bridging Asp205 of naphthalene dioxygenase sits at. So they occupy the equivalent position. Whether they do the equivalent job cannot be concluded here, for two reasons. Both structures contain a single subunit, so the neighbouring cluster the bridge would reach is simply absent from the file. And replacing Asp205 with glutamate in naphthalene dioxygenase abolishes the route to the active-site iron while leaving the NADH-to-Rieske step intact, so a naturally occurring glutamate at the equivalent position is a puzzle rather than a confirmation. Resolving it needs a structure of one of these enzymes with the oligomer intact.
