Rieske oxygenases: one catalytic architecture, many chemistries

A Rieske oxygenase alpha subunit carries two metal centres in one chain. A Rieske [2Fe-2S] cluster at the N-terminus accepts electrons from a ferredoxin, passes them across the subunit interface, and a mononuclear Fe(II) site at the C-terminus uses them to activate O2 and attack the substrate. Every one of the 11,422 proteins in this database was confirmed to have both centres intact, so what differs between them is not the machinery but what they point it at.

1.2 · The two carboxylates behave differently

Writing both of these positions as "D/E" hides the sharpest sequence-level result in the database. The carboxylate that completes the iron triad is aspartate and effectively nothing else. The carboxylate at the subunit interface is aspartate in the ring-hydroxylating enzymes and glutamate in the quaternary-amine branch, so a single alignment column tracks the substrate chemistry.

2 · What the reaction actually is

The two metal centres support more than one chemistry. Counting types and members by reaction shows that the cis-dihydroxylation the family is named for is only the most common case, not the whole story.
